The Road Behind

30 Years of Highway.
Then the Page.

Born in Chilliwack, British Columbia into a Dutch farming family, Jac Winters spent nearly three decades behind the wheel — ice roads, custom harvesting, and over-dimensional hauls across the full breadth of North America.

Those miles were his real classroom. He learned resilience, the value of unexpected kindness, and the art of sitting with silence. When the trucking chapter closed, writing didn't just begin — it erupted. Not as a hobby, but as a calling decades in the making.

Jac now lives in Green Bay, Wisconsin, with his wife and two children. He writes for the misfits, the dreamers, and the ones who've faced the dark and kept moving anyway.

To Say Goodbye Again

Saying Farewell — A Survivor's Poetry of Loss and Renewal

Jac Winters lived for half a century in silence, carrying memories that stayed deep in his bones. In To Say Goodbye Again, he distills that long journey into powerful poetry. What began as private verses in 2017 cracked open decades of fear, confusion, and anger — revealing the arduous road to reconciliation and peace. A Titan Gold Book Award recipient, this collection offers an intimate prelude to his forthcoming memoir. Through poems born from the cab of a truck and the endless black ribbon of highway, Jac pays quiet homage to resilience, redemption, and the simple joy of trucking.

A Taste of the Road

From "To Say Goodbye Again"

I've made you sturdy enough
for one such blow as this,
so even if you prayed with all your might
to have one more dinner at candlelight…
Another boat ride or paraglide,
to live, to laugh, to cry
with all of them by your side.
Even if you mustered all your strength,
or conjured up every ounce of will to pull you through —
to say goodbye again
would kill you.
